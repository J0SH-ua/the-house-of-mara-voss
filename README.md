# The House of Mara Voss

A psychological point-and-click narrative in Godot 4.6 (C#). You are not
Mara Voss — you are the part of her that still knows something is wrong.
Examine the rooms of a house that misremembers itself, spend **Clarity**
to see objects as they truly are, learn four late-game **Memory Verbs**, and
recover six fragments — while watching the memory **corrupt** the deeper
you insist on looking. The completed route runs through all seven rooms —
plus a landing flashback, Mara's present-day apartment, a night of
towering box-hedge walls beyond the bridge, and an optional way under
the house. Both the main road and
that detour now depend on practical things carried between rooms —
hairpins, ordinary keys, lockpicks and a flashlight — before they end
at one of three endings. Dialogue never tells you how to play; the house is meant to be
figured out.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Left click | Examine / interact · advance dialogue (1st click completes the line, 2nd advances). In an active room, clicking empty air strains MEMORY (**2.0×** the old repeat-look unit; another loose click in nearly the same place is **2.3×**), and deliberately re-facing an already-faced examinable is **3.0×** |
| Right click | Release armed Clarity or cancel an armed Memory Verb (free) |
| Space / Enter | Advance dialogue. In the hedge walls, when no dialogue is showing, **Space** is Mara's jump |
| End | Skip the current dialogue sequence |
| C | Arm / release Clarity (or click the eye toggle in-room). Inside the hedge walls, C belongs to her body instead: hold it to crouch |
| E / A / T / R | **Echo / Anchor / Trace / Release** when learned — hear order, hold one truth still, connect causes, or decide a completed memory (also available from the collapsible **MEMORY** tab at the bottom). Inside the hedge walls, **A** is a movement key, not Anchor |
| Mouse + hold A (at a lock) | Lockpicking, the Dying Light way her father taught her: the pick is **locked to the mouse** and follows it exactly around the top of the lock — no sliding, no mouse button. **Hold A** (or D) to turn the cylinder. How far it turns before the pick starts to shake is the whole secret; let go and the unset layer springs back. A layer turned home clicks set with the unlocked sound of a little lock and stays set; deeper layers sit narrower. The roll holds **seven picks and no gauge**: forcing a bind does hidden, lasting damage until one snaps (costing the pick, never MEMORY), and snapping all seven brings the panic choice — spend every saved Clarity on the rewind, or let MEMORY go |
| Drag with the mouse (Room 4's brass door) | The hallway keys are worked by hand: drag a carried key from the ledge at the left into a keyhole and let go. **Any hole takes any key** — a true key turns its full, easy turn, a borrowed bed only partway; the door judges only when all three stand in it, and refuses a mixed-up set until Mara works out who is in the wrong bed. Click a placed key to lift it back out |
| Click in time (Room 3's last jar) | Mum's hairpin comes out of the bag on screen and the pin itself travels the seal's rim while the one red stretch where the rubber gives keeps moving. Click the instant the hairpin crosses the red: **seven true taps**, faster each time, direction reversing after every hit — and one mistimed tap starts the seal again |
| W (in the vent) | Hold to crawl forward; release to stop exactly where Mara is |
| WASD + mouse/arrows (in the hedge walls) | Walk and strafe through the dark box-hedge corridors in first person; move the mouse or use **←/→** to turn. **Shift** runs, **Caps Lock** looks behind her (press again to face forward), **C** crouches, and **Space** jumps. The Bagged flashlight is the light she explores by; the south opening returns to the bridge and the far north-west opening leads on |
| Mouse (with the torch in the Bag) | Aim Dad's flashlight in blackout rooms, the vent, the basement, the night hedge walls, and over anything Mara is holding |
| Tab (hold) | Reveal remaining hotspots |
| H | Hint (progressive: nudge → names the object → rings it) |
| J / M | Memory Journal — fragments, echoes, auto-noted clues (incl. puzzle arrangements), and a free notes page kept in your save |
| B / I or the satchel icon (top-right) | Mara's Bag / inventory — every fragment is a visible keepsake (key, photograph, jar, frame, letter, apple), and every practical thing gets its own illustrated, selectable card below them (Mum's hairpin, the three hallway keys, Father's lockpicks and fine wrench, the flashlight, the iron cellar key, the negatives). Fragments and practical pickups alike first rise in Mara's palm, are lifted to the light with a line of hers, and only then fly visibly into the Bag; Inserted hallway keys remain pictured with their keyhole status. One keepsake can be held to the light: select the sitting-room **photograph** and choose **Look at the photograph** — mouse wheel leans closer, drag moves over the print. And **anything selected can be taken back into her hand** (**Take it in hand**): the same palm → lift-to-the-light moment, a fresh line of hers for it now (the lockpick set even counts its sound picks by touch), and a click puts it away. For the trunk's unprinted **negatives** that in-hand look *is* their use — held to the light once in any place they steady her (MEMORY eased, Clarity +1, landing as she puts them away), and the first light through them *develops* a cellar frame only the negatives kept (their father hanging the hammer inside the tool-cabinet door), without which the bridge's mending tools simply can't be found: the unprinted past is a map. In a place whose light has spoken they can still be held and looked at — looking never cost anything. Available in every room |
| Q / Backspace | Step back — leave a puzzle for its room, or return to the previous room (also the on-screen Step Back button, bottom-left). Finished rooms you step back into are **sealed** (their examinables no longer answer — exits always do, and a practical thing she walked past can always still be fetched). A fragment alone is enough to leave any room by: if the room is still keeping something practical, Mara is asked once out loud whether she would rather *look around a little more* — leaving anyway is always allowed, and anything skipped can be gone back for. There is no "return to the new room" button anymore: the bottom-right button is **Leave** only — one room onward, the room your current fragment has opened — and trips between the neighbouring rooms, forward or back, in light or in the blackout dark, are **walked** through the hallway journeys (hold **W**, click the door) — the opened three-key brass door is the one direct threshold, clicked straight through. Going back for something means walking back for it, room by room, in silence: Mara only speaks on a first forward crossing |
| Click / Space / Enter (introduction after Start) | Advance one still at a time — each post-Start introduction caption stays readable until you continue; after the still and caption have settled, the bare words **Click to continue** (no box or background) slowly fade in beneath them; Esc, End, or Skip skips it (New Game only; Continue skips it) |
| Esc / P | Pause menu (Esc first releases Clarity or a Memory Verb if armed) |

## Design rules implemented

- **Looking has a cost, and clicking carelessly costs more.** A first look
  is free. In an active room, a loose click on empty air costs 2.0× the old
  repeat-look unit; another loose click in nearly the same place costs
  2.3×; deliberately facing an already-faced examinable again costs 3.0×.
  Deeper layers also corrode the memory until text glitches, the score
  sours and you hear a heartbeat. Dialogue, panels, HUD furniture, sealed
  rooms, journeys/cutscenes and puzzle thresholds do not punish clicks.
- **Clarity is an economy.** Spend it to see truly *and* to steady the
  memory; earn it back by finishing rooms. Spend the last of it too early
  and the house gives you fifteen seconds to finish the room anyway.
- **Clues never spell the answer.** Journal cards, objectives and all
  three hint tiers point at where the truth is kept and how to read it;
  combinations and words are only ever *read off the things themselves*
  (a stopped clock, a letter's last line). Padlock **Unlock** buttons are
  always visible and always pressable — a wrong code rattles, says so,
  and sends you to the Journal (J) instead of hiding the way forward.
- **The story is told in order, slowly.** A plain-spoken intro cutscene;
  the house's kindnesses noticed one by one; Room 4's mirror opening onto
  the landing flashback; Mara monologues at Rooms 5–7's thresholds;
  suspicion aimed where it belongs — at a memory that edits, lovingly.
- **Nothing fails silently.** Locked drawers explain themselves with
  progress (`3/7`), wrong padlock words rattle and clear, empty Clarity
  tells you how to recover it.
- **Everything read can be re-read** in the Memory Journal (J), including
  lines from examine layers that free themselves.
- **Inventory is global.** The old inventory icons were duplicated inside
  Rooms 1–3 only; Mara's Bag (B/I) is part of the persistent HUD and works
  in every room, distinguishing fragments still carried from ones returned
  and picturing practical things — tools, keys and lights — whose uses may
  be rooms away from where they were found.
- **Collection is idempotent.** Shards go through
  `GameState.CollectShard` (this fixed a double-fired brass-key handler
  and an arrow that granted the shard for free).
- **Later rooms change the verb, not just the lock.** Echo teaches order,
  Anchor teaches stillness, Trace teaches causality, and Release asks what
  a whole memory is for.
- **Dialogue does not teach.** Story lines name what wakes (Echo, Anchor,
  Trace, Release) and how it felt — never which key to press or what to
  click next. How things work lives in the visual How to Play, the Journal's
  “way of remembering” cards, and the H hints: places the player chooses
  to consult. Puzzle feedback stays in the fiction (a rising note, a line
  that breaks, a key turning in the hole it traveled rooms to find).
- **Finished rooms rest.** Step back into a room whose work is done and
  nothing in it is interactable anymore — but the way back to where you
  were is the way you came: walked, one hallway journey at a time.
- **Three endings remember the player.** Memory shatter gives the Hollow
  Ending; completing Room 7 offers **Waking** or **The Kept House**. Found
  endings persist between New Games.
